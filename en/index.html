04 — About

Two perspectives, one outcome

AI projects rarely fail on technology. They fail because nobody organises the passage from ambition to everyday practice.

I spent more than twenty years on the market side: as key account manager at an e-commerce agency, selling an AI-driven personalisation platform, most recently managing an international portfolio for compliance software across EMEA. Those years taught me one thing above all: organisations do not buy technology. They buy the resolution of a problem they often have not yet named precisely.

In 2020 I deliberately rebuilt my methodological foundation and completed thirteen certifications within roughly a year — project management, business analysis, agile methods, IT strategy. Not as a collection, but because I wanted to master the other half: not only to explain what a system can do, but to be accountable for it actually being implemented. Immediately afterwards I led the introduction of a laboratory information system as IT project manager.

That same principle — evidence over assertion — is what I applied to the technical side of AI in 2026: in a part-time AI Consultant certification program, I am deepening prompt engineering, working with AI models, and hands-on workflow automation. Each module closes with a graded practical exam — the program is due to complete in August 2026.

An AI project has clearly distinct roles: one that clarifies need and goal and translates between leadership, IT and the business; one that collects, structures and makes data available; one that connects models and interfaces into a usable application; and two more that build and statistically validate their own learning models. I fully cover the first of these roles — AI manager — with 25+ years as a bridge-builder between leadership, IT, legal and the business. I am actively building out the technical delivery side: in my AI Consultant certification program I have already completed several self-built, top-scoring practical projects — including a multimodal AI prototype and a production code contribution to a team software migration. I bring adjacent data experience from earlier roles and current projects, and continue to deepen modern data infrastructure. For building custom learning models, I partner with specialised data scientists and ML engineers. For organisations, that means an experienced point of contact for needs assessment and steering, with technical delivery capability that is now concretely evidenced — so far from training, not yet from a client engagement.

Today I advise organisations on AI strategy, process automation and digital transformation — with deep expertise in the EU AI Act, where regulation becomes a strategic question. Most organisations do not even know which AI systems they already run — and have been subject to deployer obligations since February 2025.

What interests me in this work is the moment an abstract requirement turns into a concrete list a team can pick up on Monday morning. Everything before that is preparation; everything after it is craft.

Inventory before strategy

Without knowing what is already in use, you can neither classify nor prioritise. The least glamorous step is usually the decisive one.

Regulation is not a brake

The EU AI Act forces clarity about purpose, accountability and risk. Most AI initiatives lack exactly that clarity anyway.

Measurable or not at all

A transformation project without a defined effect is a budget line. I agree on outcomes before I start.

Automated Intake and Governance Workflow for First-Contact Inquiries

Client
Confidential — real estate and financial advisory firm (name withheld at client's request)
Year
2025
Role
AI Consultant / Delivery

Situation

A real estate and financial advisory firm was drowning in first-contact inquiries that had to be manually classified, checked for missing information and prioritised — time that was missing from actual advisory work. The intake process needed to move from concept to an actually working, automated system — including error handling, monitoring and a solid economic case.

Approach

Analysed the process with SIPOC and 5-Whys, then implemented a 21-node workflow in n8n: an AI agent with structured output classifies processing risk and missing information, a switch automatically routes to review, follow-up or governance review, with dedicated error paths for every critical component.

Result

Delivered and put into production as a client engagement in 2025, with a calculated ROI in the range of roughly 131% over six months.

Hybrid AI Architecture for a Hardware Advisory Tool

Client
Training project · WBS module EIOPAN (fictional case company)
Year
2026
Role
AI Consultant certification — scored highly

Situation

Standard product filters do not reliably translate model size, quantisation, context length and use case into a sound hardware recommendation for locally run AI models — risking advice that is either wrong or altogether absent.

Approach

Hybrid architecture combining a rule-based compatibility engine, versioned fact/benchmark data (RAG) and a language model used strictly for explanation — never for the technical judgement itself. An output validator blocks invented specifications, a reproducible decision record documents every recommendation, backed by four approval gates.

Result

Rated “absolute professional quality” by the examiner. Hard quality gates fully met: zero invented specifications, 100% source coverage, at least 98% of must-have requirements satisfied. Training project from the WBS AI Consultant program, not a real client engagement.

  • AI architecture
  • RAG
  • Responsible AI
  • Quality assurance
